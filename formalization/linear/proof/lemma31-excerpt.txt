\begin{lemma}\label{lem:jacobian}
Let $B=\mathbb C[[s_1,\ldots,s_r]]$, $R=B[[z_1,\ldots,z_c]]$, and
\[
 A=R/(H_1,\ldots,H_c),
 \qquad
 \Delta=\det\left(\frac{\partial H_a}{\partial z_b}\right)\in A,
\]
where $r,c\geq1$ and the $H_a$ form a regular sequence.
Assume that $A$ is finite flat over $B$ and that $A_{\mathrm{red}}\simeq B$ as a $B$-algebra.
If $N$ is the nilradical of $A$, then
\[
 \Ann_A(N)=B\Delta.
\]
Moreover, if $\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_r\in A$ reduce to a regular system of parameters of $A/N$, then the image of $\Delta$ generates the socle of $A/(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_r)$ and is nonzero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Translate each $z_b$ by its image in $A/N=B$.
Then the kernel of $R\to A/N$ is $(z_1,\ldots,z_c)$, and differentiation in the $z_b$ with the $s_i$ fixed is unchanged.
Let $K$ be the fraction field of $B$ and put $\mathfrak p=(z_1,\ldots,z_c)\subset R$.
Since $\sqrt{(H_1,\ldots,H_c)}=\mathfrak p$, the finite local $K$-algebra $A_K=A\otimes_BK$ identifies with $R_{\mathfrak p}/(H_1,\ldots,H_c)$.
Completing $R_{\mathfrak p}$ gives $K[[z_1,\ldots,z_c]]$ and leaves its Artinian quotient $A_K$ unchanged.
This presents $A_K$ as a zero-dimensional complete intersection with residue field $K$.
The Jacobian theorem therefore gives $N_K\Delta=0$.
Since $A$ is $B$-flat, $A\to A_K$ is injective, and hence $N\Delta=0$ in $A$.

Put $M=\Ann_A(N)$.
The closed fiber $A/\mathfrak m_BA$ is an Artinian complete intersection and hence Gorenstein.
Lift a linear functional defining its perfect multiplication pairing to a $B$-linear map $\ell:A\to B$.
The $A$-linear map
\[
 A\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_B(A,B),
 \qquad a\longmapsto(b\longmapsto\ell(ab)),
\]
is an isomorphism modulo $\mathfrak m_B$ and hence an isomorphism between free $B$-modules of the same rank.
Finite adjunction consequently gives an isomorphism of $B$-modules
\[
 M=\operatorname{Hom}_A(B,A)
 \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_A(B,\operatorname{Hom}_B(A,B))
 \simeq B.
\]
The Jacobian theorem on the closed fiber gives $\Delta\notin\mathfrak m_BA$, and therefore $\Delta\notin\mathfrak m_BM$.
As $M$ is free of rank one over $B$, it follows that $M=B\Delta$.

Now let $B'=\mathbb C[[T_1,\ldots,T_r]]$ act on $A$ by $T_i\mapsto\tau_i$.
The induced map $B'\to A/N$ is an isomorphism.
Each quotient in the finite filtration by powers of $N$ is finite over $A/N$, so $A$ is finite over $B'$.
The $\tau_i$ form a system of parameters in the Cohen--Macaulay ring $A$; thus $A$ is free over $B'$.
The closed fiber $A/(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_r)$ is again a zero-dimensional complete intersection, so $\operatorname{Hom}_{B'}(A,B')$ is free of rank one over $A$ by the same argument.

The quotient $A\to A/N=B'$ splits as a map of $B'$-modules.
Dualizing gives a split injection $B'\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{B'}(A,B')$ whose image consists of the functionals vanishing on $N$.
Under an $A$-linear identification $\mathrm{Hom}_{B'}(A,B')\simeq A$, this image is precisely $\Ann_A(N)=M$.
Thus $M\hookrightarrow A$ splits over $B'$.
The actions of $B$ and $B'$ on $M$ both factor through $A/N$, so $M=B'\Delta$.
Reduction modulo $(T_1,\ldots,T_r)$ preserves the split injection.
Hence $\Delta$ remains nonzero in $A/(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_r)$.
The maximal ideal of this quotient is the image of $N$ and annihilates $\Delta$.
Its socle is one-dimensional, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}
