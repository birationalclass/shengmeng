# 数学难民营 — A retreat for wandering minds

An original, navigable 3D architectural scene for Sheng Meng's Visual Lab. **Not a video, image panorama, or 2.5D photo displacement.** The structure, rooms, furniture, plants, pool and surrounding terrain are modeled geometry. This is an initial reference-inspired scene, not a verified pixel-equivalent reconstruction of the supplied film.

## Reference and camera design

Reference: [梦境之初q — 刷到就说明你未来将会拥有这样的房子](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1umun6PEvs/), 8:09, posted 2026-08-06, labelled as including AI-generated content by Bilibili. Initial access returned 412; a later ordinary browser opening succeeded. Observed opening material includes a layered dark-framed glass hillside villa, terraces, a reflecting-water passage with stone steps, warm linear lighting, an atrium and window-side interiors. We have **not** extracted the original camera tracks, underlying models or material maps, nor verified every shot of the full film. No original frames, audio or models are redistributed.

The eight-chapter tour uses arc-length sampled Catmull–Rom camera and target curves with eased movement: coastal arrival, low pool-side dolly, library, auditorium, a dedicated chalkboard lesson, ocean terrace, rising retreat and a bamboo/creek/waterfall garden chapter. The auditorium camera follows the central aisle rather than crossing seats. The lesson camera follows the active board pair with a damped, level close view. Each chapter has its own position, target and field of view. Chapter transitions fade, manual dragging stops the tour, and resume blends back (long relocations fade rather than fly through walls). These are inspired camera categories, **not motion capture from the reference**.

## Rendering and modeling

- Complete geometric, multi-level architecture: slabs, vertical fins, mullions, open loggias, stairs and railings; rounded upholstery, tables, modeled books, brass torus-knot sculpture and mathematical blackboard.
- Two offset building wings, a glazed connecting gallery, set-back upper discussion pavilion, external stair, roof garden and cliffside viewing deck. The building and its board system now scale uniformly by √2, **doubling footprint area**, with camera paths, point-light ranges/intensity and shadow bounds adjusted consistently. Outdoor planting locations/terrain expand for clearance, while ordinary outdoor plant sizes remain in world metres. Seat, sofa and occasional-table dimensions are compensated to retain their original physical size inside the enlarged rooms. Ocean-facing doors remain actual gaps in the glazing.
- The former central conference table and face-to-face seating are removed. The ground-floor wing is now a 32-seat mathematics auditorium: four rows, two four-seat blocks per row, a wide central aisle, shallow row risers, and a compact 1.08-m-high lectern offset to the left of the blackboards. Every chair faces the writing wall. The board-close camera and free central-aisle tour remain unobstructed by the removed table. Layout metadata and code tests check the seat count, aisle clearance, orientation and building scale; no visibility/accessibility certification is claimed.
- An 8,000-unit procedural ocean with animated multi-scale water normals, Fresnel horizon color and sun glitter. Terrain drops below water beyond the east cliff, vegetation stays ashore and mountains are confined to the landward side. The ocean intentionally uses an analytic shader, not another planar-reflection camera, avoiding recursive reflection passes with the courtyard pool. Its surface is planar; it is not a hydrodynamic simulation or ray-traced ocean.
- Physically based wood and marble material maps, normal/roughness detail, physical glass material, PMREM sky lighting, shadow maps, ACES tone mapping, restrained bloom and multisample render targets.
- Close-range refinement adds world-dimension bevels (instead of stretching one rounded cube), cushion/chair seams, stone paver joints, recessed glazing seals, conference-table cable troughs and inset power lids. Reusable 128-square procedural cloth normal/roughness, brushed-metal roughness and painted-surface micro-normal maps add about 0.33 MiB including mip levels. Ceramic uses a separate glazed material rather than the fabric material; closed glass solids render front faces only to avoid double-sided transparency passes. No transmission ray tracing or displacement tessellation is claimed.
- A rendered planar reflection camera with animated water normals; reflections change with the live viewpoint. This is not ray-traced refraction or a fluid simulation.
- Seeded instancing batches for repeated elements and vegetation; finite terrain grid and mountains; capped pixel ratio and a lighter rendering setting. No frame-rate target is claimed without browser/device testing.
- The environment revision replaces the old ellipsoidal foliage and thirteen cone mountains entirely. Three seeded broadleaf prototypes use tapered, bent trunks, flared roots, branching twigs and individually modeled narrow leaves. Palms have arching frond spines and paired leaflets; planters/pool margins use fern fronds, with ground-level grass tufts and irregular rock outcrops. A modest shared vertex-shader breeze moves foliage only; reduced-motion freezes it. Cached shadows remain static and do not reproduce each leaf's tiny movement.
- 220 distant forest trees follow clustered noise, spacing, slope and land/building exclusion tests, supplemented by a deliberately denser near-site belt. Near trees use folded four-triangle leaves; beyond 75 plan units from the site origin they use larger two-triangle leaves and fewer twigs. This is **fixed site-distance detail**, not camera-adaptive LoD. Spatially partitioned instancing permits frustum culling without thousands of individual tree draw calls. Distant plants and groundcover do not cast shadows. No transparent foliage cards or alpha-discard overdraw are used.
- The garden is organized around a sheltered back/sides and an open seaward frontage: three giant trees, three jointed-bamboo groves, two turf lawns with low flower borders, near-site woodland and fern-lined banks. This is a landscape composition, not a claim of scientifically established feng-shui effects. A continuous carved watercourse descends from a roughly 28-m main waterfall (after site scaling), through a plunge pool, around the west/front garden and out to the sea. Rock buttresses frame the cascade, stones cross the creek, and a bounded 80-point splash spray adds local detail. Water uses animated ribbon shading with foam/streaks, not fluid dynamics, volumetric mist or an extra planar-reflection pass. Planting excludes the water channel; terrain and stream share the same sampled centerline.
- A continuous 1,200-unit fractal/ridged height field replaces the isolated cone silhouettes, with a one-unit-resolution coastal patch and matched boundary heights. Rocky slopes blend triplanar rock color into forest-floor textures; nearby rock objects and trunks have photographic diffuse/OpenGL normal maps. These are rasterized PBR surfaces, not scanned tree models, displacement tessellation or volumetric foliage scattering. The shoreline shader uses a small lookup derived from the actual terrain/water intersection for shallow-water color and gentle foam bands. Distance fog layers the mountain silhouettes without a volumetric pass.
- The expanded scene now assembles **1,285,708 mesh triangles, 5,110 instances and 246 scene objects**, versus 882,580 triangles before the garden/auditorium additions. Thin chair legs/arms use simple boxes instead of unnecessarily tessellated bevels, distant forest density is reduced in favor of near-site detail, and model assembly is capped at 1.3 million triangles. This is a larger scene, **not a measured frame-rate improvement**. No new photographic texture downloads are added: the six local 1K landscape JPGs still total 5,921,427 bytes (roughly 32 MiB uncompressed RGBA including mipmaps). Phone pixel-density/MSAA budgets and the vector chalk reader remain unchanged. GPU frame rate and memory have not been measured on phones.
- Free observation with OrbitControls, touch pinch/pan and keyboard translation; **no collision-controlled walking physics**. Users can pass through geometry in free observation.
- Fullscreen, hideable controls, light adjustment, tour speed and reduced-motion support. No autoplay audio.
- A visible footer fullscreen button supports standard and prefixed WebKit fullscreen, including Escape-driven state updates. Mouse rotation defaults to 22% of OrbitControls' original gain; single-finger touch uses 16.5%. Rotation sensitivity is adjustable in settings, damping settles sooner, and zoom/pan gains remain unchanged. These input settings are local to Math Refuge, not edits to the shared vendor library.
- Entrance lintel and stair-side signs read “数学难民营”. Two independent chalkboards describe the unforced periodic 3D Navier–Stokes global smoothness question and the rational Hodge conjecture (with a question mark). The NS board has a large translucent “NS” background split into twelve displaced, clipped fragments beneath the legible foreground text. The text states the questions, not a claim about research status. Formulations: [Clay NS statement](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf), [Clay Hodge conjecture](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/hodge-conjecture/).

## Six-board spectral-sequence classroom

The auditorium has three independent pairs of counter-moving boards, six surfaces total, each with its own canvas texture. Two depth-separated rails let the boards pass while exchanging upper/lower positions. The sequence writes across the three lower boards, lowers the other boards in turn and preserves the previous work above. Grain stays deterministic rather than flickering. Row-wise reveal preserves real SVG mathematical layout; it is a chalk-writing effect, **not reconstructed human handwriting strokes**.

The chalk tip now samples actual SVG ink columns, lifts over gaps/row changes and stays at the visible reveal frontier. If canvas pixel readback is unavailable it falls back to row-based motion. The shaft wears from 17 cm down to 4.5 cm while the contact tip remains anchored; short pieces are replaced between pages. At most 64 subtle falling-powder particles are recycled. A tilted felt-faced eraser makes fourteen eased horizontal sweeps with small vertical variation and lifted turns over 24 seconds; the texture removal uses the same footprint/trajectory as the 3D tool, with a coverage test across the whole board. A single incremental wipe cache avoids replaying all past clipping operations every frame. Recently wiped regions darken slightly and have a local low-resolution roughness-map change, then dry out over 12 seconds. Pause freezes wear/dust/drying; reduced-motion hides tools/powder and clears wet trails. Only changing boards upload color/roughness textures. The six 384 × 160 roughness maps add about 1.9 MiB including mipmaps. These are visual effects rather than simulated chalk adhesion or moisture physics.

`build-chalk-notes.mjs` reads the existing `study/spectral` exports and converts their formulas into 65 local SVG pages using build-only MathJax 3.2.2. Pages cover double complexes, filtrations, convergence, Hodge and Leray; the manifest retains the source TeX and section. They reproduce the exported main formula content with short context, not every proof-dialog derivation or interactive diagram. The classroom links back to the original notebook for full statements, proofs and interactions. No runtime CDN is needed. SVG formula paths avoid depending on installed mathematical fonts; Chinese labels use system Kai-style/Chinese fallbacks. See [MathJax server integration](https://docs.mathjax.org/en/v3.2/server/direct.html).

Controls: select any page, previous/next, pause/resume writing, erase/rewrite, focus the lesson camera, and slide or exchange each board pair. Manual lifting pauses writing. Reduced-motion mode displays complete static pages; page controls and direct lifts still work. Hidden tabs do not advance the lesson. Loading failures appear in the classroom status.

### Mobile clarity and rendering budget

Phones no longer automatically select the old 1×, no-MSAA preset. Fine quality allows native pixel density up to 3×, bounded by a 3.5-million-pixel small-screen budget (6 million on larger screens). A 390 × 844 CSS-pixel, 3× device stays at 3× in either orientation. On compact screens with a multisampled native framebuffer, the scene renders directly with native antialiasing and ACES, bypassing bloom/composer passes. If native multisampling is unavailable, the compositor uses hardware-limited MSAA. Flow mode remains an explicit choice; no device frame-rate guarantee is implied. Resize/orientation changes re-evaluate the budget and dispose outdated MSAA attachments. Compact shadow maps use 1024²; readable pixels take priority over bloom.

Portrait teaching frames the active board rather than retreating to fit the full pair. Board headings/body text increase to 58/42 texture pixels, short formulas have a consistent scale cap, grain is less intrusive, and mipmaps reduce minification shimmer. Only actually changed board textures upload. The optional **高清板书** layer presents local standalone SVG equations and reflowed HTML explanations, independent of the WebGL texture and postprocessing. It opens on small-screen entry to the teaching chapter; closing it is respected for that chapter. Formula size is adjustable (18–36 CSS pixels); long formulas scroll horizontally rather than shrinking illegibly. It shows a complete page while the 3D classroom retains its writing/erasing animation. Full notebook text is retained in this reader even when the physical board uses a shorter text excerpt.

To regenerate with the pinned extracted `mathjax-full` package: `node visuals/math-refuge/build-chalk-notes.mjs /path/to/mathjax-full-3.2.2`. MathJax's license is included under `assets/chalk/`.

### Contemporary techniques considered

Primary documentation reviewed on 2026-09-19:

- [Three.js WebGPURenderer](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGPURenderer.html): modern WebGPU backend with WebGL2 fallback. Not enabled here: this project currently shares pinned Three.js 0.180.0 WebGL addons and planar-reflection materials. Calling this module WebGPU-rendered would be incorrect.
- [Spark Gaussian splat LoD](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/lod-getting-started/): streamed/adaptive splat rendering can support detailed captured environments. No training-ready, geometrically consistent capture or splat asset was supplied, so Gaussian splatting is not used or simulated by ordinary points.
- [three-gpu-pathtracer](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer): progressive physically based path tracing. Not included in this version: convergence during continuous travel and target-device performance require a separate validation pass. Current lighting is rasterized PBR with planar reflection, not full path tracing/global illumination.

The requested goal “quality and detail no lower than the video” remains **unverified and not claimed achieved**. Matching every reference room and close-up would require more asset detail and visual comparison. The user explicitly selected code checks only for this iteration, so no browser interaction/visual acceptance is performed.
