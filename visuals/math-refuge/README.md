# 数学难民营 — A retreat for wandering minds

An original, navigable 3D architectural scene for Sheng Meng's Visual Lab. **Not a video, image panorama, or 2.5D photo displacement.** The structure, rooms, furniture, plants, pool and surrounding terrain are modeled geometry. This is an initial reference-inspired scene, not a verified pixel-equivalent reconstruction of the supplied film.

## Reference and camera design

Reference: [梦境之初q — 刷到就说明你未来将会拥有这样的房子](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1umun6PEvs/), 8:09, posted 2026-08-06, labelled as including AI-generated content by Bilibili. Initial access returned 412; a later ordinary browser opening succeeded. Observed opening material includes a layered dark-framed glass hillside villa, terraces, a reflecting-water passage with stone steps, warm linear lighting, an atrium and window-side interiors. We have **not** extracted the original camera tracks, underlying models or material maps, nor verified every shot of the full film. No original frames, audio or models are redistributed.

The eight-chapter tour uses arc-length sampled Catmull–Rom camera and target curves with eased movement: coastal arrival, low pool-side dolly, library, auditorium, a dedicated chalkboard lesson, ocean terrace, rising retreat and a contained offshore garden chapter. The auditorium camera follows the central aisle rather than crossing seats. The lesson camera follows the active board pair with a damped, level close view. Each chapter has its own position, target and field of view. Chapter transitions fade, manual dragging stops the tour, and resume blends back (long relocations fade rather than fly through walls). These are inspired camera categories, **not motion capture from the reference**.

## Expandable offshore campus — 2026-09-20

This revision follows layout sketch 02 and the subsequent all-offshore, wider-pool and warm seminar-room requests, with an **open-air central water court**. It is a layout/model revision, not a completed architectural engineering design.

- Cardinal directions are explicit: east is +X, north is −Z. All buildings, service paths, gardens and expansion docks sit on modeled offshore platforms. All mountain, mainland, shoreline and natural waterfall/creek geometry is removed. No support piles are generated. The sea surface sits close beneath the platform slabs; planting survives only in the contained offshore garden trays and tree wells. No offshore islands, boats or ornamental objects interrupt the eastward horizon. Sunrise is east; the simulated sun moves west during the day.
- Two low, transparent main-villa wings flank the water court. They contain an academic living room and discussion room, with smaller upper studies/seminar rooms and terraces. A detached quiet library, two residential villas and a service/tea-kitchen pavilion sit farther west. One small timber tea pavilion provides a classical accent.
- A single connected pool surrounds the dry villa platforms, traverses the open central court, then extends along both sides of the offshore hall to a due-east infinity edge. Its disjoint surface cells cover 2,232 plan-square units, or 4,464 world-square units (2.34 times the previous surface area). Exterior swimming lanes widen from 4 to 8 plan units; the central court retains its width so it does not consume the dry rooms. The east terrace grows from 2.4 to 9.8 plan units clear of the hall. Twelve modeled resort chaises have teak frames, inclined upholstery, pillows and small tables. The pool perimeter has no fence or glass railing; bridge and upper-floor handrails remain. This unfenced virtual pool is not a buildable safety specification. Real basin bottoms, side walls, coping, an overflow sheet and recovery trough distinguish the pool from seawater. This is rendered geometry, not fluid dynamics or a swimming-pool engineering specification.
- Three shallow arch bridges have modeled timber decks and curved rails; separate level companion crossings keep the route usable without climbing the arch. Code checks reject ground-floor slabs covering swimming lanes except those explicit crossings. No accessibility certification is claimed.
- The **36-seat offshore seminar hall** restores its 20-plan-unit transverse width while retaining a 4.2 m clear height. Three rows have physical rises of 0, 0.18 and 0.36 m from front to back, two seat blocks, a central carpeted stair aisle with 9 cm steps and a small lectern. Warm ivory curved wraparound shells, reclined taupe upholstery, broad soft seat cushions, headrests, champagne supports and walnut writing tablets form a quieter lounge palette. The carpet covers both floor and stepped tiers; the north opaque acoustic wall is removed to preserve the left-hand sea view. Listeners face east; the six blackboards stand at the eastern teaching front and face west. A separate operable blackout shade controls glare behind the boards. The structure is on supported platforms above actual seawater, not filled land.
- Site coordinates retain the existing √2 scale, while room clear heights and furniture use physical dimensions. The legacy board assembly is independently scaled and rotated into the compact hall. All board-focus cameras use its transformed normal. The blackboard bottom is at least 0.905 m above the floor at every lift position. The original notes, six-board alternation, chalk wear/dust and damp wiping remain intact.
- Paths, roofs, glazing gaps, stairs, furniture and books are modeled geometry. Room functions are blocked out; detailed services, engineering and every interior fixture are not complete. Free observation has no wall-collision walking physics and can pass through rooms. A shared water-height guard runs after orbit/dolly/pan, keyboard movement and automatic cameras: the eye stays at least 1 m above the higher pool surface, and the orbit target cannot sink below that surface. Looking up toward the roof is still allowed.

The enlarged hall terrace uses large-format, honed warm limestone slabs with subtle generated mineral clouding, low-contrast 6 mm joints, a restrained darker border and a single matte champagne entry threshold. It replaces the generic small-square tile grid. All intersecting architectural platforms are tessellated as nonoverlapping unions rather than stacking coplanar floors; generic fine grid lines and repeated platform-edge lights are removed. Camera near clipping increases from 0.08 to 0.2 m and shadow bias is adjusted. These are code-level anti-flicker measures; visual acceptance remains unperformed.

### Retractable roof, scene clock and planting

Four roof leaves telescope into north/south cassettes. In automatic mode they close from 06:00 to 18:00 and open at night; manual open/closed overrides persist until auto is restored. The adjustable clock starts at 08:00 and runs a simulated day in ten minutes; pause and reduced-motion support are available. Soft warm cove strips, ceiling insets and diffuse point lights become brighter at night. Environment-map refresh is throttled to half-hour steps rather than every animation frame. This is a visual daylight model, not real-location astronomy or calibrated architectural lighting.

Three actual modeled buttons beside the boards control language, roof and automatic mode. Each has a labeled face, spring-travel feedback and a state stripe; raycasting activates a deliberate short press, rejects drags and respects opaque occluders. No projected HTML console or popup choices are used for these functions. Existing page/lift controls remain in the blackboard reader panel.

Six framed specimen tree wells use small exposed soil apertures within stone borders. Layered Terminalia-inspired trees and white frangipani-inspired flowering trees supplement the existing larger garden trees; large roots have wider contained wells. The surrounding garden trays are green groundcover/paving rather than large bare-earth islands. Botanical shape references: [NParks Terminalia mantaly Tricolor](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/2/7/2763) and [NParks Plumeria obtusa](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/3/0/3072). These are original procedural approximations, not scanned botanical specimens or root-zone engineering designs.

Only the arrival-gate logo says “数学难民营”. Individual door signs identify 报告厅, 学术客厅, 讨论室, 图书馆, 研究员居所, 茶室 and 竹庭茶亭.

## Rendering and environment

- Rasterized PBR wood/stone surfaces, physical glass, procedural cloth/metal microdetail, PMREM sky lighting, cached shadows, ACES tone mapping and no bloom. Blackboard surfaces have zero emission, zero specular intensity and a muted chalk tint; button labels also use lit matte materials. Ocean glitter, pool Fresnel/reflection strength, glass specular and metal gloss are reduced. Rounded furniture retains world-sized bevels. There is no path tracing, displacement tessellation or photogrammetric building reconstruction.
- One 512² planar-reflection pass serves the entire connected pool. The 8,000-unit ocean uses analytic waves/normals, Fresnel horizon color and sunlight, avoiding another reflection camera. Its surface remains planar. The retained 128² depth lookup is uniformly deep water in the all-offshore layout; no coastline foam is generated.
- There are no terrain meshes or distant forest belts. A constant submerged seabed is retained only as layout data; it is not a visible mountain or island.
- Instanced branching trees, folded leaves, palms, ferns, grasses and irregular rock outcrops remain. Three framed giant trees, specimen trees, bamboo groves, lawns and flower borders remain on the garden platforms; the distant natural forest is removed. Natural vegetation excludes buildings, pools, the carved creek and seawater; campus planting uses separate supported garden/planter surfaces above the seabed. Leaf breeze is disabled for reduced motion; cached shadows do not track each moving leaf.
- The old natural waterfall/creek model and its source data remain in the repository for possible reuse, but are not imported or created in the live scene.
- Geometry assembly currently counts **491,720 mesh triangles, 3,756 instances and 175 root scene objects**, below the prior 1,285,708-triangle scene and the 1.3M code budget. This is a mesh-count reduction, **not measured FPS improvement**. Spatial instancing bounds draw batches; distant plants do not cast shadows. Site-distance foliage detail is not camera-adaptive LoD.
- No new photographic textures were downloaded. The six 1K landscape JPGs still total 5,921,427 bytes, roughly 32 MiB uncompressed RGBA with mipmaps. Mobile density/MSAA budgets and the independent vector chalk reader remain unchanged. GPU frame rate/memory and visual quality have not been measured in this iteration.
- Fullscreen, hideable controls, tour/light/sensitivity settings and reduced-motion support remain. Mouse rotation defaults to 22% of the original OrbitControls gain; single-touch uses 16.5%. Manual camera ownership is unchanged. No autoplay audio.
- The single arrival logo reads “数学难民营”. Separate NS and Hodge blackboards remain in the academic living villa, including the fractured translucent NS background. They state the mathematical questions rather than announcing solutions. Formulations: [Clay NS statement](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf), [Clay Hodge conjecture](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/hodge-conjecture/).

## Six-board spectral-sequence classroom

The auditorium has three independent pairs of counter-moving boards, six surfaces total, each with its own canvas texture. Two depth-separated rails let the boards pass while exchanging upper/lower positions. The sequence writes across the three lower boards, lowers the other boards in turn and preserves the previous work above. Grain stays deterministic rather than flickering. Row-wise reveal preserves real SVG mathematical layout; it is a chalk-writing effect, **not reconstructed human handwriting strokes**.

The chalk tip samples the composed page's actual ink columns, excludes empty trailing columns from its motion, and lifts across gaps/row changes. The texture reveal follows the same ink-only progression. If pixel readback is unavailable it falls back to measured text-row bounds, not the old fixed 1,380-pixel sweep. This remains an ink-guided reveal, not linguistically reconstructed stroke order. The shaft wears from 17 cm down to 4.5 cm while the contact tip remains anchored; short pieces are replaced between pages. At most 64 subtle falling-powder particles are recycled. A tilted felt-faced eraser makes fourteen eased horizontal sweeps with small vertical variation and lifted turns over 24 seconds; the texture removal uses the same footprint/trajectory as the 3D tool, with a coverage test across the whole board. A single incremental wipe cache avoids replaying all past clipping operations every frame. Recently wiped regions darken slightly and have a local low-resolution roughness-map change, then dry out over 12 seconds. Pause freezes wear/dust/drying; reduced-motion hides tools/powder and clears wet trails. Only changing boards upload color/roughness textures. The six 384 × 160 roughness maps add about 1.9 MiB including mipmaps. These are visual effects rather than simulated chalk adhesion or moisture physics.

`build-chalk-notes.mjs` reads the existing `study/spectral` exports and converts their formulas into 65 local SVG pages using build-only MathJax 3.2.2. Pages cover double complexes, filtrations, convergence, Hodge and Leray; the manifest retains the source TeX and section. They reproduce the exported main formula content with short context, not every proof-dialog derivation or interactive diagram. The classroom links back to the original notebook for full statements, proofs and interactions. No runtime CDN is needed. SVG formula paths avoid depending on installed mathematical fonts; Runtime captions are composed in locally bundled Ma Shan Zheng (Chinese subset) and Caveat (Latin), with complete Chinese/English caption translations for all 65 pages. Language changes rebuild visible pages and ink guides without changing formula TeX or the current page/lift positions. The vector reader follows the same language. Long explanations retain their full text in the reader; physical boards use up to three lines with an ellipsis. See [MathJax server integration](https://docs.mathjax.org/en/v3.2/server/direct.html).

Controls: select any page, previous/next, pause/resume writing, erase/rewrite, focus the lesson camera, toggle the sea-facing blackout shade, and slide or exchange each board pair. Manual lifting pauses writing. Reduced-motion mode displays complete static pages; page controls and direct lifts still work. Hidden tabs do not advance the lesson. Loading failures appear in the classroom status.

### Mobile clarity and rendering budget

Phones no longer automatically select the old 1×, no-MSAA preset. Fine quality allows native pixel density up to 3×, bounded by a 3.5-million-pixel small-screen budget (6 million on larger screens). A 390 × 844 CSS-pixel, 3× device stays at 3× in either orientation. On compact screens with a multisampled native framebuffer, the scene renders directly with native antialiasing and ACES, bypassing bloom/composer passes. If native multisampling is unavailable, the compositor uses hardware-limited MSAA. Flow mode remains an explicit choice; no device frame-rate guarantee is implied. Resize/orientation changes re-evaluate the budget and dispose outdated MSAA attachments. Compact shadow maps use 1024²; readable pixels take priority over bloom.

Portrait teaching frames the active board rather than retreating to fit the full pair. Board headings/body text increase to 58/42 texture pixels, short formulas have a consistent scale cap, grain is less intrusive, and mipmaps reduce minification shimmer. Only actually changed board textures upload. The optional **高清板书** layer presents local standalone SVG equations and reflowed HTML explanations, independent of the WebGL texture and postprocessing. It opens on small-screen entry to the teaching chapter; closing it is respected for that chapter. Formula size is adjustable (18–36 CSS pixels); long formulas scroll horizontally rather than shrinking illegibly. It shows a complete page while the 3D classroom retains its writing/erasing animation. Full notebook text is retained in this reader even when the physical board uses a shorter text excerpt.

To regenerate with the pinned extracted `mathjax-full` package: `node visuals/math-refuge/build-chalk-notes.mjs /path/to/mathjax-full-3.2.2`. MathJax's license is included under `assets/chalk/`.

### Contemporary techniques considered

Primary documentation reviewed on 2026-09-19:

- [Three.js WebGPURenderer](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGPURenderer.html): modern WebGPU backend with WebGL2 fallback. Not enabled here: this project currently shares pinned Three.js 0.180.0 WebGL addons and planar-reflection materials. Calling this module WebGPU-rendered would be incorrect.
- [Spark Gaussian splat LoD](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/lod-getting-started/): streamed/adaptive splat rendering can support detailed captured environments. No training-ready, geometrically consistent capture or splat asset was supplied, so Gaussian splatting is not used or simulated by ordinary points.
- [three-gpu-pathtracer](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer): progressive physically based path tracing. Not included in this version: convergence during continuous travel and target-device performance require a separate validation pass. Current lighting is rasterized PBR with planar reflection, not full path tracing/global illumination.

The requested goal “quality and detail no lower than the video” remains **unverified and not claimed achieved**. Matching every reference room and close-up would require more asset detail and visual comparison. The user explicitly selected code checks only for this iteration, so no browser interaction/visual acceptance is performed.

## Code verification

### Manual camera ownership

Canvas pointer-down takes over the camera in the document capture phase, before OrbitControls processes the gesture. Dragging, multi-touch, pointer cancellation and window blur cannot restart the tour. Drag-generated compatibility clicks on chapter/tour/board-focus controls are suppressed; a fresh button press is accepted immediately. Light/sensitivity sliders are not treated as camera drags. Orbit auto-rotation is explicitly disabled. Chapter buttons and board focus now select a stationary viewpoint instead of implicitly starting a tour. After manual takeover, only the tour button (or its deliberate Space-key shortcut) starts touring; held-key repeats are ignored. The initial page-load tour behavior is unchanged. Mode labels and canvas diagnostics update immediately on takeover.

Run `node --test visuals/math-refuge/checks.test.mjs`: **24 code checks** cover ink-gap/trailing-space motion, all bilingual captions, daytime/nighttime/manual roof states, connected/nonoverlapping pool geometry, dry-room/basin separation, bridge landings, offshore foundations and an unobstructed eastern horizon, board orientation/ceiling fit, all eight camera chapters, local assets, finite model buffers and the 1.3M-triangle budget. They also cover three seating levels and curved chair buffers, physical-button click/drag/cancel behavior, matte non-emissive boards, nonoverlapping platform unions, absence of terrain/piles, above-water camera bounds, retained unused creek data, contained planting, texture hashes, shader replacement hooks, 65 SVG pages, mobile pixel budgets, chalk/eraser motion, six-board controls and manual-camera ownership.

Scene assembly uses real Three.js geometry with mocked texture/PMREM/canvas/image services. The tests do not compile GPU shaders, render screenshots, measure frame rate or certify architectural safety. Browser/visual testing is intentionally omitted at the user's request. The reference informed the design but was not used for a full-film, side-by-side acceptance test.
