Nef Abundance: Surface Behavior

October 10, 2026

PDF ↗

This note studies the smooth surface case of the nef-abundance proof in Numerical Semiampleness of Nef Adjoint Divisors.

In this study note, we work over $\mathbb C$.

Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface with $K_X\sim0$, and let $D$ be a nef Cartier divisor with $D^2=0$. In Section 2, we additionally assume $\nef(X,D)=2$.

For a nef divisor $D$, its nef reduction is an almost holomorphic rational map $f:X\dashrightarrow Y$ with connected fibres such that $D$ is numerically trivial on the general compact fibre and $D\cdot C>0$ for every curve through a very general point not contracted by $f$. Write $\nef(X,D)=\dim Y$. A very general point lies outside a countable union of proper closed subsets. For a Cartier divisor $H$, write $E\in|H|$ if $E$ is an effective Cartier divisor linearly equivalent to $H$. If $g$ is a local equation of $E$ at $x$, define \[ \mult_x(E)=\max\{a\geq0:g\in\mathfrak m_x^a\}, \] where $\mathfrak m_x$ is the maximal ideal of $\OO_{X,x}$. This is independent of multiplication of $g$ by a unit. A nonzero section $s\in H^0(X,\OO_X(H))$ gives its zero divisor $E=(s)_0\in|H|$, and \[ \mult_x(E)=\ord_x(s). \] Conversely every $E\in|H|$ arises this way, with the section determined up to a nonzero scalar.

1. Verified Lemmas

Lemma 1.1.

Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface, let $D$ be a nef Cartier divisor with $D^2=0$, and let $A$ be an ample Cartier divisor. For every $\epsilon>0$, there are positive integers $r_n\to\infty$ such that \[ P_n:=r_n(D+A/n),\qquad P_n^2\to\epsilon^2. \]

Proof ✓ verified by Sheng

Put \[ u_n=\sqrt{(D+A/n)^2} =\sqrt{2D\cdot A/n+A^2/n^2}>0. \] Then $u_n\to0$. For sufficiently large $n$, choose $r_n$ to be the nearest integer to $\epsilon/u_n$. This integer is positive and \[ |r_nu_n-\epsilon|\leq u_n/2\to0, \qquad r_n\to\infty. \] Set $r_n=1$ for the remaining finitely many indices. Hence $P_n^2=(r_nu_n)^2\to\epsilon^2$.

□

Lemma 1.2.

Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface and let $H$ be a nef Cartier divisor. Let $r\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}$ and let $\Lambda\subseteq|H|$ be a nonempty linear subsystem. Suppose $y$ is an isolated point of its base locus and $\mult_y(E)\geq r$ for every $E\in\Lambda$. Then \[ r^2\leq H^2. \]

Proof ✓ verified by Sheng

Let $F$ be the fixed divisorial part of $\Lambda$. Choose a general $E_1\in\Lambda$ and write $E_1=E'_1+F$. Each coefficient along $F$ has its generic minimal value, so $E'_1$ is effective and contains no component of $F$. Since $y$ is an isolated base point, $y\notin\operatorname{Supp}(F)$, and hence \[ \mult_y(E'_1)=\mult_y(E_1)\geq r. \] The residual system $\Lambda-F$ has no fixed curves. Thus a general $E_2\in\Lambda$ has no component in common with $E'_1$. Consequently $E'_1,E_2$ meet properly and $\mult_y(E_2)\geq r$. The local intersection inequality and nefness of $H$ give \[ \begin{aligned} r^2&\leq\mult_y(E'_1)\mult_y(E_2) \leq i_y(E'_1,E_2)\leq E'_1\cdot E_2\\ &=H\cdot E'_1\leq H\cdot E_1=H^2. \end{aligned} \]

□

In particular, if every member has multiplicity at least $r>\sqrt{H^2}$ at $y$, the nonempty system has a base curve through $y$.

Lemma 1.3.

Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface, $H$ a Cartier divisor, $\Lambda\subseteq|H|$ a fixed nonempty linear subsystem, and $r\in\mathbb R$. Suppose that there exists a Zariski dense subset $S\subseteq X$ such that for every $x\in S$ there is $E_x\in\Lambda$ with $\mult_x(E_x)>r$. Then for every $x\in X$ there exists a divisor $E_x\in\Lambda$ with $\mult_x(E_x)>r$.

Proof ✓ verified by Sheng

We may assume $r\geq0$. Put \[ Z=\{(x,E)\in X\times\Lambda:\mult_x(E)>r\}. \] Put $m=\dim\Lambda$. In local coordinates $u,v$ on $X$ and homogeneous parameter coordinates $[t_0:\cdots:t_m]$ on $\Lambda$, the universal effective Cartier divisor $\mathcal H$ is given by \[ f(u,v,t)=\sum_{i=0}^m t_i f_i(u,v)=0, \] where the $f_i$ represent a basis of the defining section space. Let $c_{i,ab}(x)$ be the regular coefficient functions of $u^av^b$ in the local expansion of $f_i$ in parameters centered at $x$. Then $Z$ is defined locally on $X$ by the homogeneous equations \[ \sum_{i=0}^m t_i c_{i,ab}(x)=0 \qquad(a,b\geq0,\ a+b\leq\lfloor r\rfloor), \] since $\mult_x(E_t)>r$ exactly when all these low-degree coefficients vanish. Hence $Z$ is closed in $X\times\Lambda$.

Since $\Lambda$ is projective, the projection $p:X\times\Lambda\to X$ is proper, so $p(Z)$ is closed. The hypothesis says that $p(Z)$ contains $S$, and hence $p(Z)=X$.

□

Lemma 1.4.

Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface and $A$ an ample Cartier divisor. Suppose that for every $y\in X$ there is $E\in|A|$ with $\mult_y(E)>4\sqrt{A^2}$. Then, through a general point of $X$, there is an integral curve $C$ such that \[ 0<A\cdot C\leq2\sqrt{A^2}. \]

Proof ✓ verified by Sheng

We next use Lemma 1.2 to force a base curve through the marked point. Choose a sufficiently large positive integer $k$ with $k\sqrt{A^2}\geq4$. Multiplying the divisors in the hypothesis by $k$ gives members of $|kA|$ of multiplicity strictly greater than $4k\sqrt{A^2}$. For $y\in X$, put \[ \Lambda_i(y)=\{E\in|kA|:\mult_y(E)\geq \lceil(2i+3)k\sqrt{A^2}/2\rceil\}\quad(i=0,1,2). \] All three systems are nonempty for very general $y$, by the hypothesis, and their base loci are proper. If $y$ were an isolated point of $\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_0(y)$, Lemma 1.2 with $r=h_0=\lceil3k\sqrt{A^2}/2\rceil$ would give \[ 1\leq(k/h_0)^2A^2\leq\tfrac49<1, \] a contradiction. Thus $\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_0(y)$ contains a curve through $y$.

We construct a family of base curves with a dominant marked-point map. On a dense marked-point open, jet evaluation has constant rank. The systems $\Lambda_i(y)$ are projectivizations of the corresponding section-kernel vector bundles $V_i$. We have \[ \Lambda_2(y)\subseteq\Lambda_1(y)\subseteq\Lambda_0(y), \qquad \operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_0(y)\subseteq \operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_1(y)\subseteq \operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_2(y). \] A curve component of $\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_0(y)$ remains a component of $\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_1(y)$, since a proper closed subset of a surface has dimension at most one. Track such a component over the generic marked point. Its finitely many possible components are defined after a finite parameter-field extension. Spreading out and shrinking gives a smooth integral parameter surface $T$, a dominant generically finite marked-point map $y:T\to X$, and a family of integral curves $C_t$ common to $\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_0(y(t))$ and $\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda_1(y(t))$. Its incidence \[ \Gamma=\{(t,z):z\in C_t\}\subset T\times X \] dominates $X$, because $y(t)\in C_t$.

We compare parameter derivatives with the lower jet kernel. Set $h=\lceil k\sqrt{A^2}/2\rceil$. Choose local defining-section representatives from a frame of $V_1$, the section bundle underlying $\Lambda_1$. Each has the form \[ s(t,z)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha(t)s_\alpha(z), \] with $s_\alpha$ fixed global sections of $kA$. Parameter derivatives with $z$ fixed are again global sections at each fixed parameter. In coordinates centered at the moving mark $y(t)$, $s$ starts in order $\lceil5k\sqrt{A^2}/2\rceil$. Each parameter derivative lowers this order by at most one. Thus every derivative of order $a<h$ belongs to the section space $V_0(t)$ underlying $\Lambda_0(y(t))$, since \[ \lceil5k\sqrt{A^2}/2\rceil-\lceil3k\sqrt{A^2}/2\rceil \geq k\sqrt{A^2}-1\geq h-1\geq a. \] Every such derivative vanishes along $\Gamma$.

We deduce multiplicity at least $h$ along the incidence. At a general smooth point of $\Gamma$, the dominant map $\Gamma\to X$ is submersive in characteristic zero. Parameter directions therefore span its normal space in $T\times X$. Since $\Gamma$ has codimension one, choose a parameter coordinate $t_1$ transverse to it. In completed local coordinates the formal implicit-function theorem writes \[ \Gamma:\ t_1=F(t_2,z),\qquad u=t_1-F(t_2,z). \] Holding $t_2,z$ fixed gives $\partial/\partial t_1=\partial/\partial u$ to every order. Use a frame of $kA$ depending only on $z$. The vanishing of all parameter derivatives of order less than $h$ along $\Gamma$ forces all coefficients of $u^a$ with $a<h$ to vanish. Hence $s\in\mathcal I_\Gamma^h$ in the completed local ring, and faithful flatness of completion gives the same containment in the algebraic local ring. Apply this to a finite frame and shrink to a dense algebraic open of $\Gamma$.

We restrict to the fibres and intersect with $A$ to obtain the degree bound. For a general $t$, the fibre of the incidence is reduced and agrees locally with $C_t$. Restricting the containment shows that every defining section of $\Lambda_1(y(t))$ lies in $\mathcal I_{C_t}^h$ at the generic point of $C_t$. Thus every $E\in\Lambda_1(y(t))$ satisfies $E\geq hC_t$. Since $E\sim kA$ and $A$ is ample, \[ 0<A\cdot C_t\leq(k/h)A^2\leq2\sqrt{A^2}. \]

We obtain the assertion on a nonempty open subset of $X$. Shrink $T$ to a nonempty open $T^\circ$ where the fibres are integral and the bound holds. By Chevalley's theorem, the image of the dominant map $y:T^\circ\to X$ contains a nonempty open subset $U\subset X$. Every $x\in U$ lies on such a curve $C_t$, since $y(t)\in C_t$. This proves the assertion for general points.

□

2. Jet estimates

Lemma 2.1.

Let $X$ be a smooth projective K3 surface, and let $D$ be a nef Cartier divisor with $\nef(X,D)=2$. Fix an ample Cartier divisor $A$ and $\epsilon\in\Q_{>0}$ with \[ (14\epsilon)^2<\tfrac14,\qquad80\epsilon<\tfrac34. \] Let the positive integer $n$ tend to infinity, and choose positive integers $r_n$ such that \[ P_n=r_n(D+A/n),\qquad r_n\to\infty, \qquad P_n^2\to\epsilon^2. \] There exist a positive integer $N$ and proper closed subsets $Z_i\subsetneq X$ $(i\geq1)$ such that every point \[ x\in X\setminus\bigcup_{i\geq1}Z_i \] satisfies, simultaneously for every $n\geq N$, every positive integer $k$ with $kP_n$ Cartier, and every effective divisor $E\in|kP_n|$, \[ \mult_x(E)\leq4k\sqrt{P_n^2}. \]

Proof

We use Lemma 1.4.

Suppose that the asserted bound fails generically for arbitrarily large indices $n$. Fix such an index and write \[ P=P_n,\qquad r=r_n,\qquad\rho=\sqrt{P^2}. \] The ranks of jet evaluation maps are algebraic rank conditions. Outside a countable union of proper closed subsets, a violation for some $k$ therefore gives a nonzero complete jet kernel at the general point. Put $H=kP$. Lemma 1.3 with $\Lambda=|H|$ and $r=4\sqrt{H^2}$ gives a divisor of multiplicity greater than $4\sqrt{H^2}$ at every point. Apply Lemma 1.4 to the ample Cartier divisor $H$. Its nonempty open locus meets the very-general nef-reduction locus. Choose an integral curve $C$ through a point in this intersection with \[ 0<H\cdot C\leq2\sqrt{H^2}=2k\rho, \qquad 0<P\cdot C\leq2\rho. \] Choose this point sufficiently very general for the nef reduction of $D$. Since $\nef(X,D)=2$ and $D$ is Cartier, $D\cdot C>0$ implies $D\cdot C\geq1$. Moreover $P-rD=(r/n)A$ is nef. Hence \[ r\leq rD\cdot C\leq P\cdot C\leq2\rho. \] This is impossible for arbitrarily large $n$, because $r_n\to\infty$ and $\rho_n=\sqrt{P_n^2}\to\epsilon$. No higher-dimensional bounded-curve argument is needed: the proper positive-dimensional base component on a surface is already a curve.

Consequently some $N$, depending only on the fixed data and the chosen sequence, works at the general point for every $n\geq N$. Take the union of the exceptional rank loci for the countably many indices, Cartier multiples and integral jet orders, together with the exceptional loci used for the nef reduction. This gives the single countable union in the statement.

□

Here $N$ depends on the fixed data $X,D,A,\epsilon$ and the chosen sequence $(r_n)$, independently of $x$. A single very general point works for every $n\geq N$: take the union of the exceptional countable unions for the countably many indices $n$.

Corollary 2.3.

Let $X,D$ satisfy the common hypotheses above. Fix an ample Cartier divisor $A$ and $\epsilon\in\Q_{>0}$ with $(14\epsilon)^2<1/4$ and $80\epsilon<3/4$. Let $n\to\infty$ through positive integers and choose positive integers $r_n$ such that $P_n=r_n(D+A/n)$, $r_n\to\infty$, and $P_n^2\to\epsilon^2$. With $p_i:X\times X\to X$ the projections, set \[ Z=\mathbb P(p_1^*\OO_X(D)\oplus p_2^*\OO_X(D)) \xrightarrow{\pi}X\times X, \qquad\xi=c_1(\OO_Z(1)), \] with the symmetric-power convention for $\OO_Z(1)$. Fix a suitable positive integer $q$ and a sufficiently large index $n$ for which \[ R_n=\pi^*(p_1^*P_n+p_2^*P_n)+q\xi \quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon(R_n;z)>6 \] at a smooth point $z\in Z$ outside the two natural sections of $\pi$. Here $\varepsilon(R_n;z)=\inf_{C\ni z}(R_n\cdot C)/\mult_z C$. Then, for sufficiently large divisible $k_0$ with $k_0R_n$ and $k_0P_n$ Cartier, the evaluation map \[ H^0(Z,\OO_Z(k_0R_n))\longrightarrow \OO_Z(k_0R_n)_z\otimes_{\OO_{Z,z}} \OO_{Z,z}/\mathfrak m_z^{6k_0+1} \] is surjective, where $\mathfrak m_z$ is the maximal ideal of $\OO_{Z,z}$. Thus the sections generate jets through order $6k_0$.

Proof

Choose $a\in\Q$ with $6<a<\varepsilon(R_n;z)$. On the blowup $b:\widetilde Z\to Z$ at $z$, with exceptional divisor $G$, the divisor $b^*R_n-aG$ is ample. For sufficiently large divisible $k_0$, Serre vanishing gives \[ H^1(\widetilde Z, \OO_{\widetilde Z}(k_0b^*R_n-k_0aG))=0. \] The restriction sequence gives surjectivity onto the local quotient modulo $\mathfrak m_z^{k_0a}$. Taking $k_0$ also so that $k_0(a-6)\geq1$ yields $k_0a\geq6k_0+1$, and hence the stated map.

□

After a local trivialization and smooth coordinates $u_1,\ldots,u_5$ at $z$, every prescribed Taylor polynomial spanned by $u_1^{a_1}\cdots u_5^{a_5}$ with $\sum a_i\leq6k_0$ is realized. This is surjectivity for all prescribed Taylor data, rather than only existence of one section with large vanishing order. The surface arguments above supply the two auxiliary inputs for Lemma 2.1. The full higher-dimensional theorem and the remaining Frobenius comparison are outside the verification in this note.

3. The finite jet system and its first Frobenius consequence

Fix $q,n,z$ as in Corollary 2.3, and put $P=P_n$ and $R=P_1+P_2+q\xi$. Choose $k_0$ once and for all as in that corollary. Since $\dim Z=5$, a local frame at $z$ identifies its jet target with \[ J_{6k_0}(z)=\C[u_1,\ldots,u_5]/(u_1,\ldots,u_5)^{6k_0+1}, \qquad \dim_{\C}J_{6k_0}(z)=\binom{6k_0+5}{5}. \] Thus a finite collection of sections suffices: choose preimages of a basis of this quotient. All these sections, $q,n,z$ and $k_0$ are fixed over $\C$ before any reduction to positive characteristic.

The projective-bundle convention and the K\"unneth formula give \[ \begin{aligned} H^0(Z,\OO_Z(k_0R))\simeq \bigoplus_{j=0}^{qk_0} &H^0(X,\OO_X(k_0P+jD))\\[-2pt] &\quad\otimes H^0(X,\OO_X(k_0P+(qk_0-j)D)). \end{aligned} \] Let $\pi(z)=(x_1,x_2)$. Trivialize the two summands near this pair and choose the fibre coordinate $t$ so that the weight-$j$ monomial is $t^j$. Since $z$ lies off both axes, $t(z)=c\ne0$. For a tensor-product basis, a local section is $f_{j,a}(u_1,u_2)g_{j,b}(u_3,u_4)t^j$. The column of its jet matrix consists of the coefficients \[ [u_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots u_4^{\alpha_4}v^{\alpha_5}] \bigl(f_{j,a}g_{j,b}(c+v)^j\bigr), \qquad |\alpha|\le6k_0, \] where $v=t-c$. Corollary 2.3 says precisely that this finite matrix has row rank $\binom{6k_0+5}{5}$. This is a simultaneous prescription of all Taylor coefficients, not merely a section with one high vanishing order.

Once a model carrying the chosen sections and jet frames has been spread over an integral finitely generated $\mathbb Z$-algebra, the full-rank condition is preserved by inverting one nonzero maximal minor. This elementary rank statement only concerns the fixed finite system. It does not by itself supply the other geometric or uniform estimates in the Frobenius argument.

Lemma 3.1.

Let $Y$ be a smooth variety over an algebraically closed field, let $y\in Y$, and let $L$ be a line bundle. Suppose a finite space of global sections generates jets through order $m$ at $y$. For every positive integer $N$, the products of $N$ of these sections generate jets through order $Nm$ for $L^{\otimes N}$.

Proof

Choose regular parameters $u_1,\ldots,u_d$ and a local frame for $L$. For each $\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d$ with $|\alpha|\le m$, choose a section whose expansion is $u^\alpha$ modulo $\mathfrak m_y^{m+1}$. For any $\beta$ with $|\beta|\le Nm$, distribute its $|\beta|$ individual variable factors into $N$ groups of size at most $m$. This writes $\beta=\alpha^{(1)}+\cdots+\alpha^{(N)}$ with $|\alpha^{(i)}|\le m$; empty groups are allowed. The product of the corresponding sections has expansion \[ u^\beta+\text{terms of total degree greater than }|\beta|. \] Order the monomial basis of the quotient by increasing total degree. The selected products have a triangular coefficient matrix with diagonal entries one. They therefore span the full quotient modulo $\mathfrak m_y^{Nm+1}$. This proof uses Taylor coefficients and does not divide by factorials; it works in every characteristic.

□

Lemma 3.2.

Let $Y$ be a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, let $D$ be Cartier, and let $P$ be $\Q$-Cartier. Let $q,k_0>0$ be integers with $k_0P$ Cartier. On \[ Z_Y=\mathbb P(\OO_Y(D)_1\oplus\OO_Y(D)_2), \qquad R=P_1+P_2+q\xi, \] suppose a fixed finite system of sections of $k_0R$ generates jets through order $6k_0$ at a point $z$ off both axes. Choose a Cartier divisor $T$ so that every $T+aD$, for $0\le a\le(k_0-1)q$, has sections nonzero at both base coordinates of $z$. Put \[ N_p=\lfloor p/k_0\rfloor,\qquad B_p=k_0N_pP+T. \] If $p\ge6k_0$, sections of $pq\xi+B_{p,1}+B_{p,2}$ surject onto the local quotient by the $p$-th powers of five regular parameters at $z$.

Proof

Lemma 3.1 gives jets through order $6k_0N_p$ for $N_pk_0R$. Set $d_p=(p-k_0N_p)q$, so $0\le d_p\le(k_0-1)q$. Choose sections of $T$ and $T+d_pD$ nonzero at the first and second base coordinates, respectively. Their tensor product, in the weight-zero summand of the bundle formula, gives a section of $d_p\xi+T_1+T_2$ nonzero at $z$: its fibre monomial is the $d_p$-th power of the second coordinate. Multiplication by this section is invertible on every local jet quotient and changes $N_pk_0R$ into $pq\xi+B_{p,1}+B_{p,2}$.

Moreover, \[ 6k_0N_p\ge6(p-k_0+1)\ge5(p-1)\qquad(p\ge6k_0). \] Every monomial not in $(u_1^p,\ldots,u_5^p)$ has total degree at most $5(p-1)$. Hence \[ \mathfrak m_z^{6k_0N_p+1}\subseteq(u_1^p,\ldots,u_5^p). \] Ordinary jet surjectivity therefore implies the asserted surjectivity onto the Frobenius quotient.

□

Corollary 3.3.

Under Lemma 3.2, let $F:Y\to Y'$ be relative Frobenius and let $D'$ be the twist of $D$. Put $\mathcal E=F_*\OO_Y(B_p)$. Then $\operatorname{rk}\mathcal E=p^2$, and the natural evaluation map on $Y'\times Y'$, \[ \bigoplus_{a+b=q} H^0(Y,\OO_Y(B_p+paD))\otimes H^0(Y,\OO_Y(B_p+pbD))\otimes \OO(-aD'_1-bD'_2) \longrightarrow\mathcal E\boxtimes\mathcal E, \] has generic rank $p^4$. Here $a,b$ are nonnegative integers.

Proof

Smoothness makes $F$ finite flat of degree $p^2$. Let $A_*$ be the tensor product of the two local Frobenius-fibre algebras at the base coordinates of $z$. Each has dimension $p^2$, so $\dim A_*=p^4$. With the above fibre coordinate and $c=t(z)\ne0$, the five-parameter Frobenius quotient is \[ A_*[t]/(t^p-c^p). \] It is free over the possibly nonreduced algebra $A_*$ with basis $1,t,\ldots,t^{p-1}$. Project to the coefficient of $1$. For $j=pa+b$ with $0\le b<p$, reduction of $t^j$ gives $c^{pa}t^b$. Thus precisely the weights $j=pa$, $0\le a\le q$, survive. Their coefficients belong to \[ H^0(Y,B_p+paD)\otimes H^0(Y,B_p+p(q-a)D). \] The projection formula identifies these with the values of the displayed evaluation map in local frames; each multiplier $c^{pa}$ is nonzero. Lemma 3.2 says the whole quotient is spanned, so its projection spans $A_*$. The evaluation has rank $p^4$ at this base pair, hence has generic rank $p^4$. No reducedness of $A_*$ is used.

□

The preceding arguments explain the first rank calculation in Section 6 of the source paper. They are conditional on the fixed jet system, whose construction here still uses the unaudited Proposition 2.2. The remaining comparison needs a separately fixed cotangent-slope flag, a movable-curve witness on a point blowup, and estimates uniform in $p$ for diagonal ranks and determinant vanishing. These inputs are not proved in this note. In particular, no proof that $D$ has an effective numerical representative, or that the branch $D^2=0$, $\nef(X,D)=2$ is impossible, is asserted here.
